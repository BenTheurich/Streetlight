Overture geography

Overture Maps supplies roads, addresses, buildings, and retained land-use evidence. Transportation and buildings use ODbL 1.0. Addresses have provider-specific licenses. Streetlight normalizes and splits roads, infers some road names, estimates homes, assigns addresses, and selects building footprints.

The recorded release on a map identifies the applicable upstream notices, including OpenStreetMap, TomTom, Esri Community Maps contributors, Microsoft, Google Open Buildings, USGS, Qian Shi et al., IGN, and address suppliers:

USA Structures

Some U.S. maps include USA Structures, credited to Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) and the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) Geospatial Response Office, under CC BY 4.0.

The distributed source includes Esri's documented field changes. Streetlight further filters and selects single-family footprints to fill confirmed gaps in Overture buildings. Stored polygons and source identifiers remain available with the adapted geography.

Access to adapted geographic data

Streetlight offers the corresponding adapted geographic database, or complete geographic alterations, in machine-readable form under ODbL 1.0, free of charge over the internet. Source contents retain their applicable notices and licenses.

Email bentheurich@gmail.com with the map's location, release or date, and packet code if available. You do not need a Streetlight account or church membership to request this data. Geographic reuse does not require permission from Streetlight and remains governed by the supplied licenses.

The offer covers normalized road geography, names, estimates, assigned geographic addresses, and selected building footprints, including retained earlier geography. Church account information, packet assignments, and outreach or reconciliation history are separate operational records and are excluded.

The public importer and normalizer records the current transformation method. Copies use the stored geographic generation, including accepted FEMA footprints, rather than replacing it with today's source data.
